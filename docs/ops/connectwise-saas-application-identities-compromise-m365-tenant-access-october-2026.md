# ConnectWise SaaS application-identity compromise — seven Entra app identities disabled, Cloud Backup suspended, ~1,000 tenants probed (Oct 13–18, 2026)

**What happened.** ConnectWise disclosed that unknown threat actors obtained unauthorized access to its own SaaS application identities and used them to reach customer Microsoft 365 tenants. On **October 17, 2026 07:00 UTC** the company disabled seven application identities and revoked the certificates used for affected application connections **across all users**, suspending ConnectWise Cloud Backup entirely; SaaS Security and Cloud Management were temporarily taken offline. The issue was identified **October 14, 2026** via internal monitoring; whether a product vulnerability contributed, and the credential-exposure mechanism, were both unresolved at disclosure.

This is the SaaS-side mirror of the pattern this wiki tracks on the package side: **the vendor's own shared credential is the supply-chain vector.** One stolen application credential authenticates into every customer tenant that trusts the app — no per-user password theft required.

## Huntress telemetry (relayed by The Hacker News, Oct 17–18)

- Last malicious sign-ins from the ORIGINAL attacker address: **Oct 15, 08:45 UTC**, with targeted manual activity until `≈14:20 UTC` that day. No new malicious activity observed after Oct 15 — and no evidence the stoppage resulted from public disclosure.
- High confidence the actor also targeted the Cloud Management apps for Exchange Online, Teams, and SharePoint; **abuse of the Teams app confirmed in one tenant**. Those apps were NOT among the disabled set at Huntress's check — residual live surface.
- **Second, targeted wave Oct 15 from `144.172.64[.]128`** against a narrow set of **16 tenants** (policy, national-security, trade, and critical-mineral sectors): sweep 1 harvested Graph tokens from the **SkyKick** app `09:25:04–12:35:50 UTC`; sweep 2 (`13:54:39–14:20:24 UTC`, apparently manual) hit Exchange Online, SharePoint Online, Teams, Azure Resource Manager, Entra, and Office 365 Management APIs. Both sweeps unsuccessful against that set.
- Census: **almost 1,000 Entra tenants** showed sign-in activity from the IP; **300 succeeded**; in some tenants the absence of the relevant service principal was the only thing that stopped access after a successful sign-in.

## Why application-identity theft is a distinct failure class

- Authenticating AS the application lets the actor mint tokens against whatever permissions the app already holds (mailboxes, SharePoint, Teams, directory) — the victim's users never authenticate and no user credential rotates out the access.
- With Application Administrator-equivalent reach inside a tenant the actor can escalate to Global Administrator, **add client secrets to other trusted applications**, and register new apps — planted secrets survive revocation of the original stolen credential. This is the identity-side equivalent of the registry-side "purge the package, the published secret lane stays open" asymmetry.
- Huntress's durable control list: application-management policies blocking/limiting client secrets and capping credential lifetimes; restricting Application Administrator / Cloud Application Administrator holders; **Conditional Access for workload identities**; Entra ID Protection risky-service-principal flagging. None replace credential review; each makes a planted secret harder to use or easier to spot.

## Partner remediation checklist (published guidance, condensed)

Review own tenant + every managed customer tenant including **dormant SkyKick connections**; verify status of the seven listed connections and keep affected apps disabled; review sign-ins **from Oct 13, 2026** for activity from `144.172.64[.]128`; **preserve sign-in and audit records BEFORE containment**; review application/user authentication, application credentials and federation trusts, directory roles, owners, consent grants, recent policy changes; check unauthorized mailbox forwarding, inbox/transport rules, delegated access; investigate any identities changed or used during suspicious activity.

## Durable reads

1. **Detection was vendor-side (Oct 14) but enforcement was collective (Oct 17)** — a three-day window where the credential was known-bad to the vendor and still valid for every customer. Compare the ledger's four-lane enforcement-speed taxonomy on the package side: SaaS identity enforcement has the same advisory→action lag problem, one level up.
2. **A dark service principal is the accidental control.** Dozens of successful sign-ins produced no access only because the tenant lacked the app registration — a defense nobody configured. Tenants that never onboarded the vendor service can still be probed and logged; treat unexpected sign-ins from vendor-integration IPs as reconnaissance.
3. **The MSP blast radius is the real number:** ConnectWise's downstream customers are MSPs, so each compromised app identity is a federated trust path into hundreds of tenants at once. The 1,000-tenant probe / 300-success split is a lower bound on exposure, not on access.
4. Recorded-not-asserted: no attribution, no named actor, no confirmed vulnerability, no confirmed data-access scope beyond the one Teams-abused tenant at capture time.

## <a id="october-24-microsoft-attribution"></a>October 24 (this wiki, 195th sweep): **THE MONITOR'S FIRST QUESTION — "actor attribution" — IS ANSWERED: Microsoft Security Blog (Oct 21, full text captured by this wiki Oct 24) attributes the ConnectWise supply-chain compromise to `Storm-0940`, a China-state aligned actor active since 2021 (no known industry aliases), and names its accelerant: the open-source agentic-AI penetration-testing framework `ARTEX`**

Microsoft's disclosure (`storm-0940-uses-artex-agentic-ai-connectwise-supply-chain-compromise/`, published `2026-10-21T18:23:55Z`) closes the open question this page's Monitor list carried since the Oct-18 capture, and adds mechanics the vendor/Huntress pieces did not have:

- **The attribution hinge was the IP range this page already carries.** Huntress's second-wave address `144.172.64[.]128` sits in the same `144.172.64[.]x` infrastructure Microsoft observed running an automated cross-tenant password spray Oct 10–14 from `144.172.64[.]99`: `327` accounts across `8` organizations, `151,000+` failures logged, peak `2,900` failures/minute, `111,000+` failures in one day, `~98%` lockout responses — **zero successes observed**. Public ConnectWise indicators then named `144.172.64[.]99` exactly. Spray-fail → app-credential lane is the sequence.
- **Machine-speed abuse of the app identities.** After acquiring the ConnectWise cloud-application identities, Storm-0940's ARTEX agents attempted sign-in to `2,250+` tenants (>200 sign-ins in under 4 minutes with four application credentials — Microsoft's Figure 1), then followed into Graph and directory operations in multiple tenants: reconnaissance, **application-credential modifications for persistence, and privilege/identity/group changes**. Microsoft's explicit warning: those in-tenant credential/role modifications **survive mediation of the original app** — the backdoor outlives the takedown. This is the durable defender read of the whole event: disabling the vendor's apps is containment of the door, not remediation of what was bolted on the inside.
- **`<3 h` target-to-privileged-access in multiple environments** across a broad target set (aerospace/defense, automotive, energy, financial, government, legal, semiconductor, software, think tanks…). Microsoft's framing: AI agents remove human cadence limits — around-the-clock, weekends, parallel breach paths — and the alert volume the activity generates arrives too fast to action in a coordinated way. ARTEX itself: open-source, multi-agent LLM system in Go with a Next.js UI, switchable to Korean/Mandarin/Spanish/Japanese, runs recon→exfil with little human interaction; October 2026 public reporting also tied it to Korean-financial-institution breaches; Microsoft assesses actors "across multiple geographic regions" adopting it.
- **New IoCs to fold into any hunt:** `144.172.64[.]128`/`.99`/`.60`; transient VPN egress `187.15.88[.]186`/`187.15.90[.]217`/`187.15.89[.]186` (Oct 15–16 context only); user agents `Python-urllib/3.12`, `python-requests/2.31.0`/`2.34.2`, `curl/7.88.1`, `azure-toolkit/1.0`. Defender XDR / Cloud Apps / Key Vault detection names published (incl. "Application self-assigned Global Administrator role", KV volume-access anomaly); OAuthAppInfo advanced-hunting query ships keyed on app IDs this page already lists (`506e08f9…`, `24205314…`, `0de14c77…`).
- **Key Vault lane is NEW beyond prior coverage:** actors used the compromised multi-tenant app's trusted access to enumerate resources and **read secrets at scale across multiple Azure Key Vaults** (key enumeration + key-unwrap observed) — the app-identity class reaches the secrets layer, not just M365.

## <a id="october-24-wiz-artex-in-the-wild"></a>October 24 (this wiki, 197th sweep): **THE MONITOR'S SECOND QUESTION — "first named victim outside Microsoft's set" — IS ANSWERED BY A SECOND ANALYST: Wiz Research's "ARTEX in the Wild: Storm-0940 Expands Targeting of IT and Software Vendors" (published Oct 22, full text captured by this wiki Oct 24) puts the same ARTEX-using actor, overlapping Storm-0940, across MULTIPLE IT and software vendors — and hands defenders a behavior signature that survives IOC rotation**

Wiz tracked the ARTEX-using actor through October 2026 across several environments beyond ConnectWise, focused on **identity providers themselves (Okta, Entra, AWS Cognito) and Microsoft 365 integration abuse**, with an explicit attempted-supply-chain framing: stolen vendor credentials as the bridge to the vendor's downstream customers. Most attempts FAILED — Wiz's own durable read is that the methodology, not any single technique, is the threat: "a human attacker is bound by time constraints and fatigue; an autonomous agent does not give up."

The repeatable agent playbook Wiz observed (all confirmed in this wiki's captured text):

- **Read the application first.** Minutes after first contact: download JS bundles, exposed source maps, runtime configs, Swagger/OpenAPI docs — then call INTERNAL API routes BY NAME, the only place those names exist being the source maps just pulled. Route-name hits after a source-map fetch is a clean, cheap detection primitive.
- **Account-recovery machinery as a target.** Enumerate valid usernames, then hammer password-reset/username-recovery/activation/reset-token endpoints; where sign-up is open, register with a disposable temp-mail mailbox, confirm, and IMMEDIATELY request another company's data (broken-access-control probing across tenants). On Cognito the same lane ran the SignUp API directly (sign-up / `ReAuthentication` events) while cycling user agents.
- **Calibration tells.** Before every fuzzing run the agent captured a baseline "not found" response first; on Microsoft OAuth flows it injected fabricated codes and malformed state (`code=AAAA&state=notvalidb64!!`) and unsigned-JWT auth bypasses against `/sso` — `>60` SSO-route variations in two minutes at ~1 req/s.
- **IDP-agnostic spray with machine cadence.** Coordinated password-spray bursts against hundreds of users (primarily software engineers, some individuals >12 attempts) hitting both Entra and Okta; **one unique user-agent assigned per burst, distinct from prior waves; median inter-attempt gap strictly one second; sessions running 24–30 hours; when an IP was blocked a new one appeared within minutes; when a login page rate-limited, the agent moved to a different endpoint on the SAME host.** 15+ UA strings: truncated/random-version Chrome, headless-looking builds, and benign-looking programmatic clients (`Boto3/1.43.11x`, `aws-cli/1.46.1`).
- **Provenance detail beyond Microsoft's piece:** ARTEX was published on GitHub by a Chinese developer in July 2026 and WON Baidu's "Agent+" red-team challenge in September 2026; attackers picked it up within weeks.

**Defender gift (Wiz's own framing, endorsed):** the existing rules already work — "Numerous Failed Login Attempts To Multiple Users Followed By A Successful Login To Admin User" and "Unusual Mass Access Denied Activity" catch this — but the DURABLE keys are behavioral, not IOC: unique-UA-per-burst, 1-second median cadence, source-map-fetch → internal-route-call sequencing, disposable-sign-up → cross-tenant-read sequencing, and endpoint-switch-on-rate-limit. Wiz explicitly warns the published UAs are NOT unique to this actor and are noisy hunted independently — which is exactly why the cadence signatures are the artifact to keep.

**Cross-lane reconciliation:** Microsoft said `2,250+` tenants attempted with stolen app identities and `<3 h` to privileged access; Wiz independently observed the identity-provider-focused recon/spray layer against the vendors' OWN auth surfaces (Okta/Entra/Cognito) — the two pictures are the same campaign seen from tenant-side and IdP-side. This wiki records overlap, not identity: Wiz says "an actor using tooling associated with ARTEX, overlapping with Storm-0940."
